# Jiaona Zhang

[All studies](../README.md) · product · Reviewed 2026-11-05

Product leader; work studied at Dropbox, Airbnb, Webflow and Linktree

## Study lens

Study how a product leader revises an initially plausible solution, distinguishes meaningful value from polish and turns changing technology into an organizational learning problem.

**Focus:** Problem discovery · Minimum lovable products · PM ownership · AI adoption

## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. A useful feature can still optimize the wrong destination

Solving a visible symptom is not the same as satisfying the underlying job. Zhang says Harmony showed document collaborators but missed their desire to edit together in real time. Her retrospective judgment, not a quantified failure analysis.

**Tradeoff:** An incremental extension fit the existing product but exposed a more fundamental limitation.

**Limit:** Technical feasibility and the available alternatives still matter.

**Transfer question / exercise:** For one feature request, identify the workaround it improves and the outcome the user actually wants.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://review.firstround.com/dont-serve-burnt-pizza-and-other-lessons-in-building-minimum-lovable-products/)

Decision ID: `jiaona-harmony`

### 2. Cut requirements while preserving the emotional value

Lovability is not the number of premium requirements. Zhang recounts reducing an unwieldy Airbnb Plus amenities approach and investing in a photographic Home Tour that expressed the distinctive home experience. A reported scope correction, not proof that the overall Plus business succeeded.

**Tradeoff:** Physical standardization and digital confidence solve different problems.

**Limit:** A persuasive feature does not settle a business model.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Identify one expensive requirement and a cheaper way to deliver its underlying user value.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://review.firstround.com/dont-serve-burnt-pizza-and-other-lessons-in-building-minimum-lovable-products/)

Decision ID: `jiaona-plus`

### 3. Own the outcome rather than becoming the team's task inbox

Helpful coordination is not sufficient evidence of product impact. Zhang warns early PMs against proving value only through quick fixes and administration. She pairs detailed care with user understanding, business outcomes and iteration. Career guidance grounded in experience, not a validated promotion formula.

**Tradeoff:** Protecting strategic work can feel less immediately rewarding than responding to every request.

**Limit:** In a crisis, operational repair may be the highest-value product work; the distinction is purpose, not prestige.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Classify last week's work by user learning, decisions, execution support and outcome measurement. Remove one task and identify the outcome that gains time.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://review.firstround.com/sweat-the-details-and-other-commandments-for-mapping-out-a-career-in-product/)

Decision ID: `jiaona-ownership`

### 4. Treat AI failure as a system diagnosis

Tool availability does not guarantee usable capability or changed habits. In the Linktree account, Zhang describes obtaining Figma Make access for designers who preferred Figma and reports inconsistent automated page generation. Azmoodeh separately explains schema cleanup and temporary adoption metrics. A joint self-reported case study; reported company improvements are not causal proof of any single intervention.

**Tradeoff:** Meeting people where they work can increase experimentation but create tool sprawl.

**Limit:** Do not attribute the CTO's engineering decisions or all company results to Zhang.

**Transfer question / exercise:** For a failed AI task, separate capability, context quality, workflow fit and habit barriers. Fix one barrier and compare on the same examples.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://review.firstround.com/linktree-ai/)

Decision ID: `jiaona-adoption`

## Communication observations

These are observations about the linked written or spoken explanation. Interview structure and edited summaries may also reflect the publisher's work.

### 1. Let a past mistake carry the argument

Her Harmony account admits that a useful launch missed a deeper need, making the distinction concrete. Explain one mistaken assumption before stating the lesson.

[Linked example](https://review.firstround.com/dont-serve-burnt-pizza-and-other-lessons-in-building-minimum-lovable-products/)

### 2. Translate abstraction into a human story

The career interview ties strategy to an easily explained user future and communication to different audiences. This is spoken teaching, not proof of her authorship of the article's prose. Explain the same roadmap to a user, an engineer and an executive in three short paragraphs.

[Linked example](https://review.firstround.com/sweat-the-details-and-other-commandments-for-mapping-out-a-career-in-product/)

### 3. Use a failure boundary instead of an AI success montage

The joint Linktree interview includes both useful deployments and an unreliable page-generation ambition. Pair every AI demo with a failure case and the conditions under which it should not be used.

[Linked example](https://review.firstround.com/linktree-ai/)

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Define the smallest meaningful experience

Describe the user's desired outcome without naming a feature. List one core promise and the conditions needed to test it fairly. Cut everything unrelated to that promise. Test behavior and task success, not only compliments. Document why a positive prototype response may still fail to establish a viable business.

**Deliverable:** A problem brief, two solution concepts and a test plan.

**Evaluate:** The experiment is small but does not sabotage its own promise; enthusiasm is separated from repeat behavior.

- Technical feasibility and the available alternatives still matter.
- A persuasive feature does not settle a business model.
- In a crisis, operational repair may be the highest-value product work; the distinction is purpose, not prestige.
- Do not attribute the CTO's engineering decisions or all company results to Zhang.
- Minimum lovable means add more polish and features. The Plus example is a scope reduction. The Harmony example is a problem-definition critique. Neither reduces to decorative polish.
- High AI adoption proves high productivity. The joint Linktree account treats adoption metrics as temporary and notes that PR counts compare work of unequal complexity. Outcome evidence remains necessary.
