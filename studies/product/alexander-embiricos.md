## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Stop optimizing for every existing top user

Remotion narrowed toward technical teams, explicitly deprioritizing about 60% of previous top users. New-user interviews shifted the goal from inducing spontaneous conversation to improving calls already happening. Reported weekly users fell 8%, while call usage rose 26% and screen sharing 77%. Team pivot reported by its cofounder.

**Tradeoff:** Less breadth and some churn for deeper use of the chosen workflow.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/embirico_remotion-users-are-churning-but-were-jazzed-activity-7039291337925382144-uDXV)

Decision ID: `ae-narrow-segment`

### 2. Pause feature expansion to repair the core

The team canceled two in-progress features, shipped 19 significant fixes and improvements, and increased polish investment. Embiricos connects this to a narrower audience needing a few dependable interactions. Team execution change, explained in Embiricos's own post.

**Tradeoff:** Fewer visible launches in exchange for reliability and clearer value.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/embirico_after-a-5-week-shipping-spree-our-team-went-activity-7046185358585163776-qCkH)

Decision ID: `ae-quality-pause`

### 3. Join OpenAI and sunset Multi

The announcement paired the new direction with closed signups, a July 24, 2024 sunset and data-deletion date, export instructions, and an extension route. Company decision announced by Embiricos; transaction terms are not established here.

**Tradeoff:** Pursuing a new opportunity imposed switching work on existing customers.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://multi.app/blog/multi-is-joining-openai)

Decision ID: `ae-sunset-multi`

### 4. Teach delegation rather than assume users will discover it

Embiricos describes changing Codex onboarding to make multiple parallel tasks the initial discovery moment. The team designed independent execution and reviewable work rather than only an interactive typing assistant. Shared product direction and onboarding practice; the interview separately credits research work to Wang and colleagues.

**Tradeoff:** More asynchronous throughput, but greater demands on task definition, environment setup, and review.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/training-data-openai-codex)

Decision ID: `ae-onboard-delegation`

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Write the update you would send after losing users

Pick one target role and one valuable workflow. Compare overall activity, target-segment retention, completed jobs, and failure rates. Write a 250-word decision update explaining whether falling activity is healthy focus, friction, or genuine failure. Include the strongest competing interpretation and the evidence that would distinguish it.

**Deliverable:** A concise update plus an explicit metric-definition sheet.

**Evaluate:** No percentage appears without its population and time window. The selected metric tracks user value rather than flattering the strategy. The memo states what would change the decision.

### 2. Design the return path before the agent task

Choose one bounded delegated task, such as proposing metadata changes. Design the review screen first: proposed change, supporting evidence, confidence limitations, and accept/reject controls. Specify what the agent must stop and ask about, including conflicting instructions and missing permissions. Test one successful result, one plausible but wrong result, and one incomplete result with users.

**Deliverable:** A review prototype and task contract with acceptance, rejection, and recovery paths.

**Evaluate:** A user can verify the result without redoing the entire task. The agent cannot silently convert a proposal into a consequential write. Parallel work does not create hidden merge or review overload.
