## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Use the customer story before the feature review

Align functions around the change the customer should experience. At Loom, Drumright describes beginning with how creators and viewers benefit, rather than jumping immediately to AI functionality.

**Tradeoff:** Early narrative can clarify choices but can also turn into unsupported marketing.

**Limit:** The interview reports her practice; it does not prove storytelling alone caused product success.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Before scoping a feature, write what a user can now do, what they would tell a colleague and what evidence supports the promise. Ask design, support and marketing to identify contradictions before reviewing screens.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://review.firstround.com/podcast/lessons-from-loom-on-product-strategy-organizational-leadership-and-cross-functional-performance-anique-drumright-coo-at-loom/)

Decision ID: `anique-customer-moment`

### 2. Make alignment observable and revisitable

Coordinate dependencies before announcing goals, then inspect progress and strain. She describes previewing plans with partners and recurring reviews of progress, problems, energy and execution.

**Tradeoff:** A repeatable rhythm adds overhead but can reveal hidden dependency failures.

**Limit:** Self-reported status is a signal, not an objective measure of performance.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Map a launch’s dependencies and ask each owner what must change for the plan to succeed. Run a short review after two weeks. Compare confidence with completed evidence and investigate disagreements without treating color scores as rankings.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://review.firstround.com/podcast/lessons-from-loom-on-product-strategy-organizational-leadership-and-cross-functional-performance-anique-drumright-coo-at-loom/)

Decision ID: `anique-alignment-loop`

### 3. Separate personal preference from case knowledge

A reusable preference and a matter fact need different persistence rules. The interview says initial personal memory covers preferences, excludes matter-specific content and permits inspection, edits and deletion; saving requires approval and notification.

**Tradeoff:** Selective memory adds interaction cost while protecting professional boundaries.

**Limit:** This describes the September interview’s first phase. Future phases discussed there were plans, not verified shipped behavior.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Classify twenty fictional observations as reusable preference, matter-only fact, sensitive information or uncertain inference. Define storage scope, approval and deletion behavior. Test that a style preference transfers while client facts do not.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.pointblank.law/p/inside-harvey-ii-legal-ai-that-remembers-how-you-work)

Decision ID: `anique-memory-boundary`

### 4. Carry context with the work instead of rebuilding it

Use the matter or project as the unit of context, access and handoff. Harvey’s announcement describes Spaces holding documents, tasks, history and permissions, with work passed between agents and lawyers.

**Tradeoff:** A shared container reduces repetition but needs precise membership and boundary management.

**Limit:** This is a team product claim, not proof Drumright personally designed every feature or an independent security validation.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Model a project with three roles and two restricted subgroups. Trace a task from intake through draft, specialist review and completion. Test changed membership, superseded evidence and an agent output addressed to a less-privileged collaborator.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/introducing-harvey-ii)

Decision ID: `anique-work-container`

## Communication observations

These are observations about the linked written or spoken explanation. Interview structure and edited summaries may also reflect the publisher's work.

### 1. Reframe scope in the customer’s language

Her Loom explanation starts from what the creator and audience should be able to say. Rewrite a roadmap item as a before-and-after customer sentence; remove internal team names and implementation terminology.

[Linked example](https://review.firstround.com/podcast/lessons-from-loom-on-product-strategy-organizational-leadership-and-cross-functional-performance-anique-drumright-coo-at-loom/)

### 2. Name the boundary as precisely as the capability

The Harvey interview distinguishes preference memory from matter-specific information. For any persistent feature, write one example it may remember and one it must not, followed by the control available to the user.

[Linked example](https://www.pointblank.law/p/inside-harvey-ii-legal-ai-that-remembers-how-you-work)

### 3. Build the launch around repeated setup cost

The team announcement begins with the friction of restarting context for each task. Describe the repeated work in concrete verbs, then show where context persists and where it intentionally stops.

[Linked example](https://www.harvey.ai/blog/introducing-harvey-ii)

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Connect narrative to operating design

Write one customer promise with a falsifiable outcome. Name the product, design, support, policy and go-to-market dependencies. Preview the plan with dependency owners and record what must stop or change. Specify project-scoped facts versus reusable preferences and their controls. Demonstrate one successful handoff and one access-boundary failure.

**Deliverable:** A launch narrative plus a responsibility map and a memory-boundary specification for a fictional professional assistant.

**Evaluate:** The promise maps to evidence rather than aspiration. Every dependency has an owner and a check date. A user can inspect and correct persistent preferences. No case-specific data silently crosses a project boundary.
