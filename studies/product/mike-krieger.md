## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Narrow Burbn to photo sharing

Krieger and Systrom extracted photo sharing from a stalled, broader product. Their combination of photos-first design and public defaults was a concrete bet, not just generic simplicity. Cofounder decision.

**Tradeoff:** A clearer core meant abandoning other use cases.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.wired.com/2014/10/why-instagram-worked/)

Decision ID: `mk-subtract-burbn`

### 2. Stop routing every product decision through founders

As Instagram expanded, founder product reviews became a bottleneck. Krieger describes shifting toward teams developing a strong point of view and returning for a check before irreversible choices. Leadership change described in retrospect; shared with Systrom and the organization.

**Tradeoff:** More team autonomy and throughput in exchange for less direct founder control.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://blog.southparkcommons.com/p/mike-kriegers-fireside-chat-from-the-spc-archive)

Decision ID: `mk-delegate-reviews`

### 3. End Artifact after testing the remaining plausible bets

The founders tried a final list of product directions, found insufficient movement, and stopped despite passionate users and personal attachment. Joint founder decision, explained by Krieger.

**Tradeoff:** Ending a valued experience versus committing more years to weak growth.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.theverge.com/24237562/anthropic-mike-krieger-claude-ai-chatbot-artifact-web-decoder-podcast-interview)

Decision ID: `mk-end-artifact`

### 4. Launch a smaller AI product before adding every possibility

Krieger describes a minimal Cowork V1 built in ten days, after longer exploration. He also reports pre-launch rewrites when inexpensive implementation had produced excessive complexity. Collective Labs/product practice described by Krieger, not a claim he personally built Cowork.

**Tradeoff:** Earlier real-world learning versus enterprise expectations for completeness and reliability.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://every.to/podcast/transcript-how-to-build-an-agent-native-product)

Decision ID: `mk-reduce-ai-v1`

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Write a subtraction brief

Write the product's essential user outcome in one sentence. For each feature, identify the behavior that shows it helps achieve that outcome. Make a prototype with half the surface area, without removing necessary permissions, recovery, or access controls. Compare task completion and comprehension. Document who becomes worse off and why the narrower version may still be worthwhile.

**Deliverable:** Two prototypes and a cut/keep/defer memo with evidence.

**Evaluate:** The smaller product preserves a complete user job. Cuts remove complexity rather than merely hiding it. The memo names excluded users and costs.

### 2. Define the final experiment before extending a project

Choose a stalled feature or side project. List the remaining hypotheses that could materially change your decision. Select the smallest discriminating test and state the evidence needed to continue. Write stop, continue, and narrow conditions before observing results. Include transition costs for existing users.

**Deliverable:** A one-page continuation decision with a date and explicit success conditions.

**Evaluate:** The test can change the decision. User affection and sufficient sustained value are measured separately. Stopping includes a responsible user transition.

- The accounts are retrospective and largely self-reported; success does not prove the described choice caused the outcome.

- undefined Analyst transfer limit: a consumer social product's public defaults are not a template for private enterprise data. Transfer the explicit tradeoff analysis, not the default.
- undefined The Labs account itself counters the idea that cheap code guarantees a good product: extra functionality made testing and explanation harder.
- undefined An acquisition, a shutdown, and a growth story do not establish a universal product rule. Founder goals, financing, distribution, and market timing can change the rational decision.
