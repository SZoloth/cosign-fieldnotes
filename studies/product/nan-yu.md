## Study lens

Study how an operating philosophy becomes both a team practice and a product capability: evidence becomes candidate projects, projects acquire explicit finish lines, and repetitive coordination becomes a shared agent workflow.

**Focus:** Continuous discovery · Product operating systems · Quality · Team agents

## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Accumulate evidence before the planning meeting

Move synthesis into everyday work rather than treating quarterly planning as a blank-page exercise. Yu describes gathering related requests and interview notes into candidate projects, while keeping severity-based bug triage separate. Candidates are later weighed against goals and commercial impact. A documented operating practice, not a controlled comparison of roadmap quality.

**Tradeoff:** Better preparation requires ongoing curation; request volume alone can privilege vocal customers.

**Limit:** Internal products need appropriate impact measures instead of copying revenue weighting.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Create three candidate projects from ten feedback items. Record the underlying problem, contradictory evidence, missing knowledge and one reason not to prioritize each.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://linear.app/now/continuous-planning-in-linear)

Decision ID: `nan-candidate-projects`

### 2. Define different kinds of done

Separate discovery, limited release and general release rather than giving a project one ambiguous completion flag. Nan describes milestones as a way to define completion at successive stages. The team interview also distinguishes concise context-setting specs from exhaustive implementation instructions. The source describes the process; it does not quantify its effect.

**Tradeoff:** A crisp boundary improves accountability but can hide unfinished adoption or operational work if milestones track only code.

**Limit:** Engineering-led projects reflected a small, experienced team. Copy the clarity, not an assumed staffing ratio.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Write separate exit criteria for an internal pilot and broad rollout. Include user success, support readiness and rollback, not only implementation.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://linear.app/now/how-we-run-projects-at-linear)

Decision ID: `nan-finish-lines`

### 3. Protect the person doing the work

Evaluate a requested capability by what it does to the core user's workflow, not only who requested it. The original interview's published takeaways describe resisting management-driven customization that worsens individual-contributor work. They also describe exploring extreme versions of a solution before converging. Reported philosophy, not evidence that every customization request should be refused.

**Tradeoff:** Opinionated defaults can produce coherence while excluding legitimate organizational requirements.

**Limit:** Use the full interview to investigate nuances beyond its editorial summary. Relevant chapters: 15:31, 44:46 and 51:38.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Sketch the fastest and safest versions of a bulk-edit flow. Compare failure recovery and user effort before choosing a compromise.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/linears-secret-to-building-beloved-b2b-products-nan-yu)

Decision ID: `nan-expert-workflow`

### 4. Turn private automation into an inspectable team system

Shared recurring work needs shared instructions, execution history and ownership. The Loops announcement presents scheduled and event-triggered agents operating with workspace context. Teams can inspect configuration and runs, rather than depending on one employee's personal script. A launch announcement verifies the intended design, not reliability or customer impact.

**Tradeoff:** Central visibility improves continuity but expands the importance of access control and governance.

**Limit:** The post explicitly says governance controls will continue expanding. Do not read a launch as proof that governance is complete.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Specify one recurring coordination task: trigger, input sources, permitted actions, owner, audit record and conditions requiring human review.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://linear.app/now/introducing-loops)

Decision ID: `nan-shared-automation`

## Communication observations

These are observations about the linked written or spoken explanation. Interview structure and edited summaries may also reflect the publisher's work.

### 1. Start with a recognizable failure of work

The planning essay opens with the disorientation of switching from execution to strategy, then gives a concrete alternative. It earns the process before naming it. Describe the reader's existing frustrating moment before introducing your proposed framework.

[Linked example](https://linear.app/now/continuous-planning-in-linear)

### 2. Explain the job before the mechanism

The Loops launch moves from specific recurring responsibilities to the shared agent concept, then configuration and examples. Order a launch narrative as job, limitation, new ability, control and example.

[Linked example](https://linear.app/now/introducing-loops)

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Build a decision-ready opportunity register

Gather anonymized observations, requests and failures. Group by underlying job rather than requested UI. Record dissenting evidence and what would change the decision. Choose one project and specify pilot and rollout completion criteria.

**Deliverable:** Three evidence-backed candidate projects and a one-page selection memo.

**Evaluate:** A teammate can explain why one project wins, why another waits and what evidence could reverse the choice.
