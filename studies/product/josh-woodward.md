## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Pair personalization with visible escape hatches

Treat correction and non-personalized use as first-class paths. The launch describes opt-in app connections, source explanations, corrections, temporary chats and regenerating without personalization.

**Tradeoff:** Additional controls introduce choices that users must understand.

**Limit:** These are announced behaviors, not independently audited privacy or reliability guarantees.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Design a personalization settings screen and a wrong-inference recovery flow using fictional data. Test whether users can identify the connected source, correct the inference and complete the same task without personalization.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/personal-intelligence/)

Decision ID: `josh-personal-control`

### 2. Explain failure with a plausible counterexample

A limitation becomes actionable when readers can recognize it. Woodward illustrates over-personalization with photos that suggest a hobby but actually reflect supporting a family member.

**Tradeoff:** A memorable example can illuminate a class of error but cannot quantify its frequency.

**Limit:** One example does not establish coverage across sensitive topics or changing relationships.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Write three false-inference scenarios for a recommendation feature. For each, distinguish observed behavior, inferred preference and confidence. Specify when the product should ask, abstain or offer a correction.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/personal-intelligence/)

Decision ID: `josh-boundary-examples`

### 3. Expand capability without losing the product’s center

Describe what remains constant before asking users to absorb a rebrand or new surface. The rename keeps a standalone research product while adding source-grounded code execution and syncing notebooks across Google surfaces.

**Tradeoff:** Distribution can increase utility while complicating identity, permissions and support.

**Limit:** The announcement states rollout plans; it does not prove feature availability for every account today.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Draft a migration brief for a research tool gaining an execution feature. Name the stable job, changed behavior, eligibility, permissions, failure recovery and support route. Have an existing user explain what changed in their own words.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-notebook/notebooklm-gemini-notebook/)

Decision ID: `josh-preserve-job`

### 4. Join user demand to newly feasible capability

Use both workflow demand and technical feasibility to choose expansion. The NotebookLM team reports requests for more languages and mobile access; model capability expanded the language plan.

**Tradeoff:** Broadening access competes with depth in existing workflows.

**Limit:** This is Johnson and Mohan’s team account. Reported growth after launches is not a controlled causal estimate or Woodward’s sole accomplishment.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Build a matrix of five user requests against feasibility, underserved audience, measurable outcome and operational cost. Pick one small test. State what adoption evidence would justify expansion and what result would stop it.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/developing-notebooklm/)

Decision ID: `josh-feedback-capability`

## Communication observations

These are observations about the linked written or spoken explanation. Interview structure and edited summaries may also reflect the publisher's work.

### 1. Lead with an ordinary moment

The Personal Intelligence post opens with a tire-shop task before explaining the feature. Write a launch opening around a specific interruption, needed information and completed outcome. Avoid a capability list in the first paragraph.

[Linked example](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/personal-intelligence/)

### 2. Separate continuity from change

The rename announcement explains the enduring research role before listing upgrades. For a major release, write separate sentences for the unchanged promise, new capability and rollout boundary.

[Linked example](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-notebook/notebooklm-gemini-notebook/)

### 3. Attribute the mechanism to its operators

The team retrospective names the people explaining feedback and technical expansion. In a case study, identify who observed the signal, who changed the plan and what remains uncertain. Do not collapse all work into a leader story.

[Linked example](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/developing-notebooklm/)

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Write a trust-aware AI launch

Open with one credible user job rather than a benchmark. Explain the new capability and the product behavior that stays stable. Map each personal or organizational source to permission, visibility, correction and deletion behavior. Add two realistic failure cases and a non-personalized fallback. Propose a staged test separating discovery, successful use and repeat use.

**Deliverable:** A 500-word launch note, a control map and a measurement plan for a fictional research assistant.

**Evaluate:** Readers can explain both benefit and limitation without reading support docs. Promised availability is distinct from future plans. Privacy statements describe specific behavior rather than absolute safety claims. Metrics distinguish distribution gains from actual task improvement.
