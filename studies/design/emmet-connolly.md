## Study lens

A principle is useful when it resolves a real tradeoff; an interface is useful when its modality matches the task.

**Focus:** Product principles · AI interfaces · Conversational systems

## What was actually reviewed

Three primary artifacts read: two bylined essays and one official transcript. The contrast across 2016 and 2024 illustrates evolving interface choices without assuming an undocumented personal change of mind.

This is a bounded source study, not an assessment of the person's entire career. Reported results, team decisions, and editorial interpretations should not be treated as interchangeable evidence.

## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Codify decision intent rather than make process the final authority.

Documented organizational preference; tradeoff analysis

**Alternative:** Require an identical workflow for every project.

**Tradeoff:** Principles allow adaptation but demand judgment; process is easier to audit and can protect novices. The tradeoff is editorial analysis of his argument.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.intercom.com/blog/product-principles)

Decision ID: `emmet-connolly-decision-1`

### 2. Test a proposed principle by writing its plausible opposite.

Documented method

**Alternative:** Adopt agreeable slogans that cannot resolve disagreement.

**Tradeoff:** A sharp preference makes tradeoffs explicit but can be wrong in another company or phase.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.intercom.com/blog/product-principles)

Decision ID: `emmet-connolly-decision-2`

### 3. Give bots a clear identity and an escape route to a human.

Documented 2016 recommendations; contemporary application is analysis

**Alternative:** Imitate human behavior or keep the user inside automation at all costs.

**Tradeoff:** Transparency and recovery can reduce frustration, but human escalation requires operational capacity. His narrow-interaction advice is historical, not a current model limit.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.intercom.com/blog/principles-bot-design)

Decision ID: `emmet-connolly-decision-3`

### 4. Use a structured form inside an AI interaction when fields are the efficient representation.

Documented 2024 scenario; not a reported controlled experiment

**Alternative:** Ask for each address field in a separate chat turn.

**Tradeoff:** A widget reduces turn-taking but requires a safe rendering system and may constrain unexpected input. These implementation consequences are analysis.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.intercom.com/blog/videos/off-script-ep04-emmet-connolly-future-of-product-design-with-ai)

Decision ID: `emmet-connolly-decision-4`

## Communication observations

The product-principles essay contrasts empty agreement with a preference whose opposite is reasonable, so the reader can evaluate their own wording.

The bot essay names concrete behaviors and closes by admitting that future capabilities or experience may invalidate its rules.

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Practice the judgment

Choose a customer-service workflow. Write three principles with reasonable opposites, then build both a chat-only and a mixed form/chat version of its most tedious step.

**Deliverable:** A two-prototype comparison, an escalation map, and a one-page principle document separating enduring user needs from capability-dependent assumptions.

**Evaluate:** Can each principle choose between two credible alternatives? Compare task completion, correction cost, and number of turns. Show users what is automated and how to recover. Identify which conclusions would change if model reliability improved.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The 2016 bot essay predates modern LLMs; its interaction constraints should not be presented as timeless.
- These are first-party arguments and examples, not independent performance evaluations.
- The 2024 piece is an edited spoken transcript with a separate introduction, not an essay written solely by Connolly.
