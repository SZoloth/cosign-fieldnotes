## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Exclude navigation redesign from an otherwise broad UI reset.

Documented decision in Saarinen's section

**Alternative:** Change application chrome and information architecture together.

**Tradeoff:** Lower engineering and habit-disruption risk at the cost of leaving a known problem for later.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)

Decision ID: `karri-saarinen-decision-1`

### 2. Explore complete screens and flows before converging on a visual direction.

Documented exploration method

**Alternative:** Resolve isolated components without testing their fit across the product.

**Tradeoff:** Cross-product coherence requires more exploration; it can surface relationships missed by local optimization.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)

Decision ID: `karri-saarinen-decision-2`

### 3. Tie a design reset to a larger product shift and leadership commitment.

Author's management recommendation; downside is analysis

**Alternative:** Treat it as discretionary visual polish.

**Tradeoff:** Strategic sponsorship enables cross-team changes but concentrates influence and requires explicit scope safeguards.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://linear.app/now/a-design-reset)

Decision ID: `karri-saarinen-decision-3`

### 4. Keep agent operation inside a structured work environment.

Author's product direction

**Alternative:** Rely on generic conversation and disconnected exports.

**Tradeoff:** Persistent context and review fit repeated workflows; a dedicated workbench may be excessive for one-off questions.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://linear.app/now/design-for-the-ai-age)

Decision ID: `karri-saarinen-decision-4`

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Practice the judgment

Audit a mature workflow for design debt. Propose a reset, then explicitly exclude one tempting but high-risk change. Draw three complete flows, including an edge case.

**Deliverable:** A one-page reset proposal, a scope exclusion, three linked flows, and a risk register.

**Evaluate:** Is the change tied to a user or product need rather than fashion? What existing habit is protected? Can the team explain why the excluded change was deferred?
