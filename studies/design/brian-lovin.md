## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Require an observable baseline and verification loop before optimization.

Author's recommended workflow

**Alternative:** Ask an agent to make an application faster or prettier without measurable acceptance criteria.

**Tradeoff:** Instrumentation costs time but helps distinguish improvement from persuasive claims.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://brianlovin.com/writing/give-your-agent-a-laboratory-jH5ryjC)

Decision ID: `brian-lovin-decision-1`

### 2. Benchmark combinations rather than assume which processing step will help.

Reported experimentation; caution is analysis

**Alternative:** Choose a library or model through intuition alone.

**Tradeoff:** Search can expose surprising options, but a weak benchmark can optimize the wrong outcome.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://brianlovin.com/writing/give-your-agent-a-laboratory-pt-ii-KjFnCW9)

Decision ID: `brian-lovin-decision-2`

### 3. Reframe critique around a useful deliverable rather than a fixed hourly promise.

Documented revision following early customers

**Alternative:** Sell a narrowly timed visual cleanup regardless of what the product needs.

**Tradeoff:** Outcome framing supports depth but requires careful scope and expectations.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://brianlovin.com/writing/design-critique-for-fun-and-profit-7wDWkhw)

Decision ID: `brian-lovin-decision-3`

### 4. Give direct-to-article visitors contextual onboarding.

Documented Staff Design change

**Alternative:** Assume every reader saw the homepage or launch announcement.

**Tradeoff:** A conditional introduction aids newcomers while avoiding repeated interruption for returning visitors.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://brianlovin.com/writing/my-playbook-for-shipping-side-projects-n0Qmhqt)

Decision ID: `brian-lovin-decision-4`

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Practice the judgment

Choose a small product defect. Define a baseline and a quality rubric before asking an agent to improve it. Require three hypotheses and an evidence-backed comparison.

**Deliverable:** A benchmark or visual test set, a before/after report, and a short retrospective about one rejected approach.

**Evaluate:** Can someone reproduce the claimed improvement? Does the rubric cover failure cases rather than only the happy path? Are human judgments separated from measured results?

## Limits and counterexamples

- The reports are first-person accounts; underlying benchmarks were not independently reproduced.
- The audio speed and cost findings must not be generalized to other speech, languages, models, or billing systems.
- The consulting experiment benefited from an existing audience and does not establish a universal pricing strategy.
