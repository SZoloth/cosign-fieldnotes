## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Stop pursuing 42 Pages when the prototype no longer supported the original product thesis.

Documented retrospective; tradeoff analysis

**Alternative:** Keep polishing a familiar manual-design interface because substantial work had already been invested.

**Tradeoff:** Stopping avoids sunk-cost escalation, but a capability-dependent rejection may become worth revisiting as models improve.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.aidesignfieldguide.com/articles/nate-parrott)

Decision ID: `nate-parrott-decision-1`

### 2. Treat chat as a baseline to beat on a specific task rather than reject it categorically.

Documented position from summer 2025, not universal prescription

**Alternative:** Build a custom interface merely to look different from chat.

**Tradeoff:** Familiar flexibility is valuable, while repeated structured operations can justify dedicated controls.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.aidesignfieldguide.com/articles/nate-parrott)

Decision ID: `nate-parrott-decision-2`

### 3. Use HTML and reusable brand context, then broaden the product framing when colleagues used it for more than mockups.

Documented first-person development account

**Alternative:** Restrict the tool to the initial design-prototyping category.

**Tradeoff:** A general visual medium enables unplanned uses but risks a less focused product and uneven output types.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://claude.com/blog/how-the-product-designer-who-built-claude-design-uses-it-to-explore-ideas-before-building-them)

Decision ID: `nate-parrott-decision-3`

### 4. Use direct editing and purpose-built controls for fine adjustments.

Documented practice; reconciliation cost is analysis

**Alternative:** Prompt again for every small alignment, color, or timing change.

**Tradeoff:** Manual control can reduce ambiguity, but users must understand the controls and the system must reconcile edits with later generation.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://claude.com/blog/how-the-product-designer-who-built-claude-design-uses-it-to-explore-ideas-before-building-them)

Decision ID: `nate-parrott-decision-4`

### 5. Keep Ball narrowly focused on a playful physical interaction.

Observed project scope; editorial interpretation

**Alternative:** Expand a small toy into a broad utility or customization platform.

**Tradeoff:** A clear scope makes the experience easy to grasp, while intentionally offering little conventional utility. This interpretation comes from the README, not a claimed business strategy.

Evidence: [Source 1](https://github.com/nate-parrott/ball)

Decision ID: `nate-parrott-decision-5`

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Practice the judgment

Choose a speculative AI feature and identify the one belief that would kill it if false. Test that belief with the smallest usable prototype. Compare chat with one direct control, and recruit another person to try using it for a purpose you did not prescribe.

**Deliverable:** A hypothesis, a low-cost prototype, a stop/continue decision, and a record of any unexpected use.

**Evaluate:** Did you test demand or merely demonstrate technical possibility? What did you avoid building? What specific failure does the custom control solve? Which conclusion would change after a model upgrade? Can the prototype be used without its maker narrating every step?

## Limits and counterexamples

- The interview was published in March 2026 but conducted in summer 2025; its model observations belong to that earlier period.
- The Claude Design article is first-party guidance and now contains later product updates despite its original July date; it is not a frozen launch specification.
- Ball is evidence of a small authored project, not a claim about commercial success or a comprehensive design philosophy.
- Interview editing belongs to the interviewers as well as the speaker. No product or code was tested.
