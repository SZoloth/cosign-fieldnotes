## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Teach the outcome rather than navigation

Early self-serve onboarding emphasized keyboard navigation. Prioritize actions that help users reach Inbox Zero. Vohra reports activation rose from 40% to 50%, a 10-percentage-point or 25% relative increase.

**Tradeoff:** Less comprehensive interface instruction; stronger immediate connection to the desired result.

**Limit:** No experiment protocol or uncertainty interval is supplied.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Which first-session instruction teaches mechanics without helping a user complete their real job?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://substack.gauravvohra.com/p/obsessing-over-onboarding-for-10)

Decision ID: `gv-d1`

### 2. Remove a successful-looking waitlist

Superhuman’s waitlist outlasted its supply constraints. Allow ready customers through instead of maintaining scarcity as a brand device. He reports roughly 3% seven-day paid conversion from a six-month-old waitlist cohort versus 10% from the live funnel.

**Tradeoff:** Lose exclusivity rituals while recovering high-intent demand.

**Limit:** An observational comparison described by the operator, not a randomized causal estimate.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What actual constraint justifies waiting, and what condition would retire the queue?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://substack.gauravvohra.com/p/the-waitlist-delusion)

Decision ID: `gv-d2`

### 3. Choose a usable activation proxy

The team needed a measurable indicator of first value. Use emails cleared and a practical time threshold, informed by retention analysis. He describes choosing 50 emails cleared within seven days; no causal uplift is claimed for choosing this threshold.

**Tradeoff:** A tractable KPI simplifies a continuous behavior and can encourage local optimization.

**Limit:** Predictiveness does not prove that forcing the action creates retention.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Can you describe the underlying value without mentioning the threshold?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://substack.gauravvohra.com/p/the-aha-moment)

Decision ID: `gv-d3`

### 4. Evaluate invitation flow separately

A team-invite step was challenged because it would not improve activation. Retain it for team expansion rather than claim activation benefits. Vohra says it drove 40% of all team invites without improving activation.

**Tradeoff:** An extra step can serve another goal but must justify its friction.

**Limit:** Invitation share is not proof of incremental revenue or durable teamwork.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Which apparently neutral experiment may affect a different valuable outcome?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://substack.gauravvohra.com/p/the-aha-moment)

Decision ID: `gv-d4`

### 5. Practice making, not only collecting examples

Frameworks and production tools are widely available. Have growth practitioners write copy and scaffold flows themselves, then ask for specific feedback. No quantified outcome supplied.

**Tradeoff:** Requires deliberate practice rather than merely delegating all craft.

**Limit:** Resonance judgments can be subjective and audience-dependent.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What can you make this week that exposes a gap between your taste and your execution?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://substack.gauravvohra.com/p/taste-resonance)

Decision ID: `gv-d5`

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Map the first-value journey

Observe a consenting tester attempting one real task. Record setup, first useful result and the next natural return trigger. Avoid prompting until they get stuck.

**Deliverable:** A journey map with three friction observations and one proposed change.

**Evaluate:** Defines a user outcome, not a screen view. Preserves consent and necessary controls. Explains what observation would disprove the proposed fix.

### 2. Write a waitlist exit rule

For a hypothetical constrained launch, identify capacity, quality and service limits. Specify a measurable condition for removing each restriction.

**Deliverable:** A one-page access policy and expiry decision.

**Evaluate:** Does not use hype as the only justification. Separates interest from retained usage and payment. Explains the cost of making high-intent users wait.

### 3. Test reach versus resonance

Write two launch messages for the same product promise. Ask readers what they remember and what action they believe the product enables.

**Deliverable:** Two drafts and an evidence-based revision.

**Evaluate:** No inflated claims. Compares comprehension and memory, not only reactions.
