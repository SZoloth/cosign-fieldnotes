## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Sequence easy wins before expensive bets

A new growth effort needed credibility. Start with tractable PQL experiments, then expand after returns diminish. He describes early results earning support for larger experiments.

**Tradeoff:** Quick learning can postpone structural work.

**Limit:** Retrospective account, not a controlled test of sequencing.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What small test would establish enough evidence to justify a larger investment?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.reforge.com/blog/freemium-product-future-of-growth)

### 2. Offer alternative sales interactions

Upgrade interest led to a callback form. Test chat, scheduling and calling options. He reports 50% higher conversions, then another 20% from in-app scheduling.

**Tradeoff:** More service capacity was required.

**Limit:** Relative improvements; baseline and uncertainty unspecified.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Where does purchase intent encounter an unnecessary wait?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.reforge.com/blog/freemium-product-future-of-growth)

Decision ID: `kfl-d2`

### 3. Change acquisition with the product

The existing inbound playbook underperformed for freemium CRM. Emphasize transactional search rather than assume educational content would convert similarly. He describes adapting the playbook; no numerical uplift supplied.

**Tradeoff:** Narrower intent coverage may sacrifice broad awareness.

**Limit:** Not proof educational content never works for CRM.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What purchase behavior makes this channel appropriate?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.intercom.com/blog/podcasts/hubspots-vp-of-marketing-and-growth-kieran-flanagan-on-product-led-growth)

Decision ID: `kfl-d3`

### 4. Give engineering a problem to solve

Marketing requests prematurely specified solutions. Articulate the problem and learn implementation constraints. He describes changing his collaboration approach.

**Tradeoff:** More discovery before execution.

**Limit:** No measured performance effect.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Can the request explain user friction without prescribing an interface?

### 5. Declare where leadership will intervene

He wanted hands-on involvement without reviewing everything. Name quarterly areas of deep involvement and invite escalation elsewhere. An operating practice, not a quantified improvement.

**Tradeoff:** Autonomy depends on people surfacing issues; he acknowledges gaps.

**Limit:** May fail where escalation is unsafe or unclear.

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Design an experiment escalation ladder

For a hypothetical collaboration product, propose one low-cost diagnostic, one medium-size intervention and one structural bet. State how each result changes the next decision.

**Deliverable:** A three-step plan with cost, decision rule, expected learning and guardrail metrics.

**Evaluate:** Measures a user or business outcome, not activity alone. Includes a stop condition and a plausible negative result. Does not mistake an easy test for the highest-value long-term strategy.

### 2. Challenge a borrowed channel playbook

Compare two hypothetical products serving the same buyer but different jobs. Draft their purchase journeys and explain why one familiar acquisition tactic might work differently.

**Deliverable:** Two short journey maps and a channel-fit hypothesis for each.

**Evaluate:** Distinguishes problem education from active buying intent. Names evidence that would overturn the channel choice. Includes servicing cost and retained use, not only signup volume.

### 3. Rewrite an engineering request

Rewrite “add a pop-up to increase upgrades” into a problem brief. Include the affected cohort, observed difficulty, business hypothesis, constraints and unanswered questions.

**Deliverable:** A 200-word brief with two solution candidates left open.

**Evaluate:** Separates evidence from assumption. Leaves room for engineering and design judgment. Protects comprehension and voluntary choice.

### 4. Practice a concise decision story

Record a two-minute explanation of a hypothetical failed experiment. Explain expectation, observation, revised belief and next test. Rewrite it after a listener identifies what was unclear.

**Deliverable:** Before-and-after scripts plus three concrete revisions.

**Evaluate:** Uses one concrete contrast and no invented results. Explains a tradeoff rather than only naming a framework. Treats disagreement as information, not disloyalty.
