## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Let expertise challenge the manager’s taste

Bonaci initially doubted a teammate’s creative campaign. Allow the teammate’s conviction and expertise to carry the bet. He says the teammate proved him wrong; no isolated revenue effect is supplied.

**Tradeoff:** Delegation can reveal ideas the leader would reject, while requiring budget and risk boundaries.

**Limit:** This is a leadership anecdote, not a rule to approve every strongly held opinion.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What evidence would justify delegating a decision despite your personal preference?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://exit-5.webflow.io/podcast/how-ramp-is-rebuilding-marketing-around-ai-agents)

Decision ID: `gb-d1`

### 2. Build shared context before automating isolated tasks

Different growth teams needed overlapping knowledge. Combine central infrastructure with team-specific adaptation. Bonaci describes the operating model, without a comparative productivity study.

**Tradeoff:** Reuse improves consistency but shared errors and excess permissions can propagate.

**Limit:** Permission boundaries, evaluation and human approval are additional requirements, not validated by the interview.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Which context should be shared, and which data or actions must remain restricted?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://exit-5.webflow.io/podcast/how-ramp-is-rebuilding-marketing-around-ai-agents)

Decision ID: `gb-d2`

### 3. Measure indirect effects instead of abandoning rigor

Direct mail and events can affect later behavior without a tracked click. Specify the expected effect and observation window before spending. He proposes incrementality testing and agreed longer horizons.

**Tradeoff:** Broader measurement captures possible halo effects but increases attribution uncertainty.

**Limit:** Reach and engagement still do not establish incremental profitable growth.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What comparison would distinguish campaign impact from customers who would convert anyway?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://exit-5.webflow.io/podcast/how-ramp-is-rebuilding-marketing-around-ai-agents)

Decision ID: `gb-d3`

### 4. Keep each stage of an experiment’s claim separate

A recap describes an agent-incentive test as successful. Read the team report’s separate stages: retrieval, mention and action. The team report says mentions grew, while human or agent action had not yet occurred.

**Tradeoff:** A narrower conclusion is less dramatic but more useful for investment decisions.

**Limit:** These are reported observations from the publication window, not a claim that no later conversions occurred.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What exact behavior must happen before you can honestly say this experiment worked?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://builders.ramp.com/post/marketing-to-ai-agents) · [Source 2](https://news.bulldozer-collective.com/p/how-ramp-runs-growth-like-a-science)

Decision ID: `gb-d4`

### 5. Check instrumentation before explaining performance

One variant initially missed major assistants because of bot-category targeting. Inspect how treatment reached each intended platform. The report discloses the error and uneven content surfacing.

**Tradeoff:** Instrumentation checks consume time but protect against false conclusions.

**Limit:** This weakens clean causal comparison between formats; it does not prove one format is universally superior.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Could an apparent treatment failure actually be a delivery or measurement failure?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://builders.ramp.com/post/marketing-to-ai-agents)

Decision ID: `gb-d5`

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Audit a success claim against its source

Compare the science-lab recap with Cummins’s experiment report. Build a ladder from exposure to retrieval, mention, action and economic value.

**Deliverable:** A source-linked claim ledger with supported, unresolved and overstated conclusions.

**Evaluate:** Credits Cummins and the team. Separates visibility from conversion. Preserves the observation window.

### 2. Design a safe expert-assistance workflow

For a fictional internal workflow, specify reusable context, task-specific judgment, access boundaries and the actions requiring approval.

**Deliverable:** A workflow specification and five failure tests.

**Evaluate:** Does not upload real confidential data. Includes traceable sources and uncertainty. Includes an audit trail and rollback or escalation.

### 3. Measure an indirect marketing effect

Use a hypothetical direct-mail campaign to define a comparison group, outcome window, total cost and guardrails.

**Deliverable:** An incrementality test brief.

**Evaluate:** Separates direct attribution from causal effect. Includes spillover and selection risks. Does not treat a memorable anecdote as ROI.
