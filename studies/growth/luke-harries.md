## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Scale the launch to the change

Small teams often reserve marketing for major releases. Use different scopes for product launches, major features and smaller improvements. The guide provides a method, not controlled evidence of incremental launch lift.

**Tradeoff:** Repeated communication requires restraint and a meaningful customer promise.

**Limit:** His cadence recommendation should not be treated as a universal release quota.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What launch treatment is proportionate to this change’s customer impact?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://harries.co/launch-your-product)

Decision ID: `lh-d1`

### 2. Define an outcome before creating assets

Assets can drift into company narrative rather than user benefit. Specify audience, core claims and an adoption goal first, then build the assets. The Scribe example sets a goal of 500 production WAU in the first week; it is a goal, not a reported result.

**Tradeoff:** Demands agreement with engineering on what the product can actually deliver.

**Limit:** Do not publish the target as achieved performance.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What observable customer behavior would make this launch worthwhile?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://harries.co/launch-your-product)

Decision ID: `lh-d2`

### 3. Hire proof of execution before narrow expertise

An early growth role spans positioning, copy, design and channel discovery. Favor a capable generalist and assess work samples plus relevant practical tasks. Harries cites successful former founders and PMs; no comparative hiring dataset is supplied.

**Tradeoff:** Less depth in one channel in exchange for independent execution across uncertain needs.

**Limit:** He explicitly allows channel or product context to change the first-hire profile.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What task would reveal the exact capability your current bottleneck requires?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://harries.co/first-growth-hire)

Decision ID: `lh-d3`

### 4. Use a tool or data asset instead of scaled filler

Large volumes of generated pages can fail to satisfy the searcher. Choose useful page types and solve the query before optimizing conversion. The guide describes the approach and examples, not guaranteed ranking results.

**Tradeoff:** Giving useful value without a gate may reduce immediate signup capture.

**Limit:** Organic traffic is not equivalent to qualified acquisition or profit.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Could the searcher complete their task on this page without becoming a lead?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://harries.co/seo-for-startups)

Decision ID: `lh-d4`

### 5. Vet who actually performs agency work

A capable agency principal may delegate through multiple contractor layers. Inspect the individual execution capability and bring proven channels in-house when appropriate. He recounts an agency managing a contractor who managed more contractors.

**Tradeoff:** Outsourcing buys speed but adds coordination and principal-agent risk.

**Limit:** A cautionary anecdote, not proof that all agencies underperform.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Who owns the work quality after the sales pitch ends?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://harries.co/first-growth-hire)

Decision ID: `lh-d5`

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Make a release brief

Choose a hypothetical feature. Define its audience, one promise, proof, launch tier, two channels and a post-launch review.

**Deliverable:** A one-page launch brief plus a 30-second demo outline.

**Evaluate:** The claim is demonstrable. The outcome is not just impressions. The effort matches the release size.

### 2. Design an intent-serving page

Pick one real query relevant to a fictional product. Compare an article, tool and template as possible answers.

**Deliverable:** A page wireframe and a rationale for the chosen format.

**Evaluate:** Completes a user job. Separates qualified visits from raw traffic.

### 3. Create a fair work-sample rubric

Translate an early growth role into three observable skills and a short, bounded assessment using a hypothetical product.

**Deliverable:** A task brief and scoring rubric.

**Evaluate:** Tests job-relevant work. Avoids unpaid production work for the hiring company. Explains the tradeoff between generalist and specialist.
