## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Replace a generic tour with task scaffolding

Airtable’s flexibility created high cognitive load for new builders. Use questions that construct a relevant workflow visually, followed by continuing education. She reports a combined 20% activation lift across the onboarding program.

**Tradeoff:** More guided structure can help novices but should not obstruct users who already know their task.

**Limit:** Not a 20-percentage-point claim and not attributable solely to the wizard.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Does the user need to learn the interface, or do they need help constructing their first useful object?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/mastering-onboarding-lauryn-isford)

Decision ID: `lm-d1`

### 2. Segment on the barrier to learning

Job-role labels did not fully explain onboarding needs. Study database familiarity and building style, and add diagnostic metrics alongside the north star. She describes better activation from learning-style segmentation; no isolated uplift given.

**Tradeoff:** More dimensions increase complexity but distinguish sophistication from teamwork.

**Limit:** Low activation percentages are not inherently desirable; predictive validity matters.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Which observed difference changes the assistance someone needs?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/mastering-onboarding-lauryn-isford)

Decision ID: `lm-d2`

### 3. Preserve valuable single-player use

Some products are useful alone even when collaboration offers additional value. Introduce relevant collaborative possibilities contextually rather than force the same invite step on everyone. The essay describes Notion exploration and reports more than 10% multiplayer activation lift for an Airtable flow.

**Tradeoff:** Slower invitations can preserve trust and the original use case.

**Limit:** Do not transfer that uplift to another archetype or infer paid expansion.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Is collaboration required, beneficial or genuinely optional for this user’s job?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.fishmanafnewsletter.com/p/crossing-the-chasm-from-single-to-multiplayer)

Decision ID: `lm-d3`

### 4. Unify the customer journey across commercial motions

Self-serve users could become enterprise customers, creating attribution tension. Treat broader and deeper usage as a shared progression; coordinate lead definitions and sales handoffs. The recap describes analysis of incremental sales revenue, without publishing the calculation.

**Tradeoff:** May cannibalize a local team’s revenue category while helping the company.

**Limit:** Product activity is a signal, not automatic permission to contact users or proof of purchase intent.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What would make a sales conversation useful to the customer at this stage?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.pocus.com/blog/product-led-sales-pls-ama-lauryn-isford-airtable)

Decision ID: `lm-d4`

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Classify a multiplayer feature

Choose three products and classify a specific use case as collaboration-required, collaboration-beneficial or collaboration-optional.

**Deliverable:** Three classifications with counterexamples.

**Evaluate:** Classifies use cases, not only brand names. Protects legitimate private use.

### 2. Design an invitee journey

Contrast a workflow creator, a reviewer and an invited contributor. Map each person’s first useful action.

**Deliverable:** Three small onboarding flows and a shared account-level outcome.

**Evaluate:** Does not make invitees recreate the workspace. Makes permissions explicit.

### 3. Decompose an activation metric

Break a team-retention metric into observable components. Add one diagnostic and one guardrail.

**Deliverable:** A metric tree with definitions and denominators.

**Evaluate:** Distinguishes correlation from causal intervention. Does not optimize a low activation rate for its own sake.
