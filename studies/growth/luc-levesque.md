## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Evaluate impact rather than visible effort

A strategy presentation did not settle whether work was changing outcomes. Connect reviews and plans to the intended result. Levesque recounts the management lesson, not a measured culture intervention.

**Tradeoff:** Outcome focus can clarify priorities but must account for uncertainty and delayed effects.

**Limit:** Avoid judging individuals solely by noisy outcomes outside their control.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What evidence would show useful progress beyond the volume of activity?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/leveraging-growth-advisors-hiring)

Decision ID: `ll-d1`

### 2. Keep an internal owner for external expertise

An advisor or agency can supply knowledge that the company cannot execute or retain. Pair expert help with an internal operator and explicit learning transfer. Presented as a preferred staffing model, not a comparative experiment.

**Tradeoff:** Slower initial capability building can reduce permanent dependence.

**Limit:** The right structure depends on budget, risk and the specificity of the bottleneck.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What should the team be able to do independently when the engagement ends?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/leveraging-growth-advisors-hiring)

Decision ID: `ll-d2`

### 3. Match search work to the content system

A few editorial pages differ from a marketplace producing many useful pages. Distinguish content creation from optimization of an existing large surface. He contrasts site types; no universal timetable is established.

**Tradeoff:** Different mechanisms imply different bottlenecks and learning periods.

**Limit:** Never infer profitable acquisition from search traffic alone.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Does the product naturally generate useful public information, or must the company create it?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/leveraging-growth-advisors-hiring)

Decision ID: `ll-d3`

### 4. Capture feedback before memory distorts it

An employee identified omissions in an annual review. Introduce brief monthly self-reviews and discussions. Levesque reports easier reviews and fewer surprises; no controlled measure is supplied.

**Tradeoff:** More frequent attention reduces recall dependence but adds process and potential rating pressure.

**Limit:** Employee experience, fairness and administrative cost require separate evaluation.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What lightweight record would make an important later decision less dependent on memory?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://luclevesque.com/blogs/luc-levesques-blog/how-monthly-performance-reviews-changed-the-way-i-lead-teams-forever-employee-review-template-included)

Decision ID: `ll-d4`

### 5. Discuss expectations instead of merely distributing them

New colleagues had to infer a leader’s preferences by trial and error. Write a short blueprint and explain it in a one-on-one conversation. The author reports positive reactions without systematic evaluation.

**Tradeoff:** Explicitness can reduce uncertainty, but a one-sided manual can reinforce power imbalance.

**Limit:** Treat the document as revisable and invite reciprocal preferences.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Which expectation is currently implicit, and how could the other person safely challenge it?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://luclevesque.com/blogs/luc-levesques-blog/leader-blueprint-how-to-help-new-employees-be-rockstars-a-new-approach)

Decision ID: `ll-d5`

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Write a two-way working agreement

Draft a one-page working agreement for a fictional collaboration. Separate required outcomes, negotiable preferences and questions for the other person.

**Deliverable:** A document plus a five-question conversation guide.

**Evaluate:** Does not turn personality quirks into performance standards. Invites correction and reciprocal needs. Names a review date.

### 2. Design a feedback record

Create a short monthly reflection for a hypothetical project with evidence, uncertainty, learning and requested support.

**Deliverable:** A reusable template and one synthetic example.

**Evaluate:** Avoids false numerical precision. Records context rather than only a rating. Uses minimal necessary personal information.

### 3. Scope an advisory engagement

For a fictional acquisition bottleneck, define the expert question, internal owner, knowledge-transfer artifact and exit criterion.

**Deliverable:** A one-page engagement brief.

**Evaluate:** Does not assume advice substitutes for execution. Defines an observable capability transfer. Separates research goals from guaranteed outcomes.
