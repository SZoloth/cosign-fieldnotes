## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Measure retained value at a natural cadence

Product use frequency differs by job. Measure cohorts on a value-producing action and appropriate interval rather than a generic login count. He cites saving content for Pinterest and ordering food for Grubhub as different operationalizations.

**Tradeoff:** Requires a defensible action definition and enough time to observe repeat behavior.

**Limit:** Retention alone does not establish acquisition economics or willingness to pay.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Would the chosen cadence misclassify a satisfied but infrequent user?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.caseyaccidental.com/p/caseys-guide-to-finding-product-market-fit)

Decision ID: `cw-d1`

### 2. Delete confusing features

Pinterest features added complexity without sufficient product value. Remove Likes, Place Pins and certain attribution elements rather than force adoption. Winters describes this as valuable work; no numeric deletion uplift is disclosed.

**Tradeoff:** Sacrifice some existing usage and development investment to simplify the core experience.

**Limit:** This is his team account, not evidence that low usage alone warrants deletion.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What would you lose by removing a feature, and what evidence shows it helps the whole product?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.caseyaccidental.com/p/feature-product-fit)

Decision ID: `cw-d2`

### 3. Add decision-relevant information despite visual density

Grubhub users struggled to choose restaurants on mobile without opening multiple tabs. Add cuisine, delivery estimates, minimums and ratings to results. He reports improved conversion and retention, without effect sizes.

**Tradeoff:** A denser screen can reduce task uncertainty.

**Limit:** The data and study protocol are not published.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Which apparent clutter actually eliminates expensive uncertainty in the task?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.caseyaccidental.com/p/feature-product-fit)

Decision ID: `cw-d3`

### 4. Disable convenience until it is reliable

Users using current location converted worse than those typing an address. Turn the component off while correcting accuracy. The essay identifies an accuracy issue; no isolated post-fix result is given.

**Tradeoff:** Remove a shortcut rather than accept unreliable task completion.

**Limit:** Segment differences alone do not establish causality; research supplied the explanation.

**Transfer question / exercise:** When should reliability outweigh one fewer step?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.caseyaccidental.com/p/feature-product-fit)

Decision ID: `cw-d4`

### 5. Invest in new value when existing growth mechanisms saturate

At Eventbrite, adding more acquisition mechanisms made the model increasingly complex. Model the limits of existing growth and examine genuinely new value propositions. He describes realizing new products were necessary; does not present this diagnosis as proof of a successful specific launch.

**Tradeoff:** Expansion diverts resources and can take years to mature.

**Limit:** A second product’s independent traction may still be too small to matter at company scale.

**Transfer question / exercise:** How large must a new product become to change the parent business’s trajectory?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.caseyaccidental.com/p/second-products)

Decision ID: `cw-d5`

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Audit one feature for fit

Choose an existing feature. Specify its retained users, adoption route, effect on the core job and a plausible harm to the wider product.

**Deliverable:** A keep, revise or retire recommendation with missing evidence called out.

**Evaluate:** Does not equate usage with value. Names the affected segment. Includes an appropriate guardrail.

### 2. Build an expansion threshold

For a hypothetical mature product, estimate current growth limits and the minimum impact a proposed adjacent product must achieve.

**Deliverable:** A sensitivity model with three explicit assumptions.

**Evaluate:** Separates users, revenue and contribution margin. Shows what happens if the new product only shifts existing usage.

### 3. Rewrite a feature announcement

Replace a feature-led announcement with a short description of the user problem, demonstrated benefit and relevant audience.

**Deliverable:** A 120-word draft and one sentence explaining when not to send it.

**Evaluate:** Targets a real user segment. Does not imply an unmeasured outcome.
