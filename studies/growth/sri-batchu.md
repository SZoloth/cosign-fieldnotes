## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Pair volume with efficiency

Cheaper acquisition can attract less valuable customers. Evaluate growth alongside contribution-margin payback rather than CAC alone. He explains the method; no comparative company uplift supplied.

**Tradeoff:** Requires assumptions about variable cost and revenue, but makes them more immediate than distant lifetime value.

**Limit:** Payback still depends on cohort behavior, cash timing and assumptions.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Could the cheapest customer be the most expensive customer to serve?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/lessons-from-scaling-ramp-sri-batchu)

Decision ID: `sb-d1`

### 2. Give an expensive strategy a credible test

Weak executions can cause teams to repeat the same unresolved idea for years. For larger strategic tests, test a strong version before optimizing cost. Batchu describes applying this to account-based marketing at Ramp.

**Tradeoff:** A bundled treatment improves the chance of detecting the strategy but obscures component attribution.

**Limit:** Even a strong null result may reflect execution or insufficient power; it cannot prove a tactic never works.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What specific question will this test settle, and which questions will it leave unresolved?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/lessons-from-scaling-ramp-sri-batchu)

Decision ID: `sb-d2`

### 3. Turn repeated operational friction into product work

Customer support and growth operations can scale headcount faster than the business. Build product solutions and reusable systems while using external tools for non-strategic work. Batchu says automated outbound became the largest share of pipeline at its peak; no underlying data published.

**Tradeoff:** Requires engineering investment and judgment about which work is truly strategic.

**Limit:** Pipeline is not closed revenue; outreach compliance and user trust remain separate constraints.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Which repeated failure should become a product fix rather than another permanent manual process?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://operatorjournal.substack.com/p/why-ramp-won)

Decision ID: `sb-d3`

### 4. Preserve risk discipline while pursuing growth

More generous credit offers can win prospects while increasing loss exposure. Maintain underwriting discipline even when competitors offered higher limits. The author attributes sustainable segment reach to this choice; no isolated causal estimate.

**Tradeoff:** Forgo some immediate acquisition to protect the economic model.

**Limit:** This is a historical business account, not financial or underwriting advice.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Which hidden cost could make a growth win economically harmful?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://operatorjournal.substack.com/p/why-ramp-won)

Decision ID: `sb-d4`

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Compare acquisition cohorts economically

Use invented data for two channels with different CAC, service costs and retention. Compute contribution-margin payback and state assumptions.

**Deliverable:** A small spreadsheet and a decision memo.

**Evaluate:** Labels synthetic data. Does not use revenue as contribution margin. Includes sensitivity to retention and cost.

### 2. Design an informative strategic test

Take a hypothetical channel strategy. Specify the strongest feasible treatment, control, measurement window and what a null result would mean.

**Deliverable:** A test protocol and an ambiguity checklist.

**Evaluate:** Does not claim certainty from low power. Separates strategy validation from component optimization.

### 3. Audit an operational loop

Identify a repeated support issue in a fictional workflow. Compare product correction, documentation and human assistance.

**Deliverable:** A decision matrix with expected cost and quality effects.

**Evaluate:** Does not automate away necessary judgment. Includes escalation and quality checks.
