## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Ask what people need before choosing the offer

Interest in AI productivity was broad and initially underspecified. Capture an open-ended motivation alongside signup and use recurring needs to shape a services offer. He reports building an agency around requests for workflow implementation help.

**Tradeoff:** Qualitative demand can guide the offer but may overrepresent vocal respondents.

**Limit:** The account does not establish total costs, profits or representative demand.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What question would reveal a real task instead of a preference for your proposed solution?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://latecheckout.substack.com/p/the-best-65-ive-ever-spent)

Decision ID: `gi-d1`

### 2. Buy a contextually timely placement

A rapidly spreading public project offered an inexpensive ad slot. Purchase the slot while its attention was growing. He reports a $65 placement and 1.2 million impressions.

**Tradeoff:** High upside from timing, with limited predictability or repeatability.

**Limit:** Impressions are not incremental signups, customers or profit.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What evidence would distinguish a lucky placement from a repeatable channel?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://latecheckout.substack.com/p/the-best-65-ive-ever-spent)

Decision ID: `gi-d2`

### 3. Define community through participant behavior

Large follower counts can be mistaken for an interacting community. Ask whether conversation continues without the brand constantly broadcasting. Presented as a diagnostic; no controlled result claimed.

**Tradeoff:** Building member relationships is different work from producing more content.

**Limit:** Communities require moderation and maintenance and do not sustain themselves automatically.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Would members have a reason to interact if the product team stopped posting?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://latecheckout.substack.com/p/audiences-versus-communities)

Decision ID: `gi-d3`

### 4. Prefer relevant impressions to maximum impressions

Broad posting can optimize platform attention rather than meaningful professional connection. Narrow the audience to people interested in the work and ideas he wants to pursue. He describes a resulting personal connection, not a general conversion rate.

**Tradeoff:** Potentially fewer views, with better fit for relationships and opportunities.

**Limit:** Do not copy artificial urgency or intrusive outreach rules.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Who should remember your work, and what should they remember it for?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://latecheckout.substack.com/p/how-i-met-my-heroes)

Decision ID: `gi-d4`

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Separate audience from community

Choose a public, consent-respecting example. Identify broadcaster-to-member and member-to-member interactions.

**Deliverable:** A behavioral map and two unanswered questions.

**Evaluate:** Does not infer trust from follower count. Accounts for moderation and maintenance.

### 2. Write a demand-discovery question

Draft a signup question that invites concrete recent behavior rather than agreement with a product pitch.

**Deliverable:** One question, three example response patterns and a coding rubric.

**Evaluate:** Avoids leading wording. Separates expressed interest from willingness to pay.

### 3. Audit a growth headline

Take a hypothetical claim combining a small spend and a large outcome. List omitted inputs and the evidence needed for causal attribution.

**Deliverable:** A rewritten honest headline and evidence checklist.

**Evaluate:** Includes labor and pre-existing distribution. Distinguishes reach, conversion, revenue and profit.
