## Decisions and tradeoffs

The source supports the documented observation or reported choice. Proposed alternatives, inferred costs, and transfer questions are editorial synthesis unless explicitly attributed.

### 1. Activate teams, not a pile of isolated accounts

SurveyMonkey had many individual accounts inside companies without successful enterprise expansion. At Miro, emphasize collaboration and help new users find existing teams. Verna contrasts stalled enterprise sales with collaborative demand; no causal effect size is disclosed.

**Tradeoff:** Joining a team can add coordination and permission complexity.

**Limit:** Individual popularity does not imply organizational willingness to buy.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Which participant handoffs create shared value rather than merely more seats?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.elenaverna.com/p/hey-b2b-i-bet-you-are-measuring-activation)

Decision ID: `ev-d1`

### 2. Give invitees a different journey

A creator, reviewer, operator and implementer may play different roles. Route users to their contribution instead of restarting setup for each signup. Presented as an operating recommendation with product examples.

**Tradeoff:** More conditional onboarding and identity/permission handling.

**Limit:** Joining by domain must still respect authorization and information boundaries.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What must an invited user do that the workspace creator never needs to do?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.elenaverna.com/p/hey-b2b-i-bet-you-are-measuring-activation)

Decision ID: `ev-d2`

### 3. Revisit the remembered growth loop

Dropbox is widely associated with storage-for-referrals. Distinguish its personal referral history from B2B sharing and marketing mechanisms. Her account names file sharing and marketing as major current drivers in 2024.

**Tradeoff:** A famous playbook is easier to retell than a segmented present-day model.

**Limit:** No relative channel contribution dataset is published.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Are you borrowing a tactic from the wrong customer segment or era?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.elenaverna.com/p/growth-at-dropbox)

Decision ID: `ev-d3`

### 4. Separate metric ownership without hiding system effects

Growth work spans several linked outcomes. Assign distinct responsibilities while connecting them through product, data and infrastructure teams. The article describes the organization rather than proving it is the optimal structure.

**Tradeoff:** Local ownership can create blind spots unless cross-metric effects are reviewed.

**Limit:** Do not copy a large-company structure into a small team.

**Transfer question / exercise:** Who notices when conversion improves but qualified acquisition or retention deteriorates?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.elenaverna.com/p/growth-at-dropbox)

Decision ID: `ev-d4`

### 5. Diagnose before selecting the familiar tactic

Teams repeatedly propose launches, channels, pricing changes and communities. Identify the underlying problem and select a fitting intervention. This is a critique and decision heuristic, not an actual experiment result.

**Tradeoff:** Harder conversations replace immediately assignable projects.

**Limit:** A satire about misuse is not evidence that the named tactic never works.

**Transfer question / exercise:** What observation says this is the constraint rather than the most fashionable available task?

Evidence: [Source 1](https://www.elenaverna.com/p/the-growth-playbook-everyone-keeps)

Decision ID: `ev-d5`

## Practice

Original exercises inspired by the sources, not practitioner-prescribed methods.

### 1. Separate four types of progress

For a fictional collaboration product, define setup, first shared value, repeated team value and payment as separate events.

**Deliverable:** An event map with distinct denominators.

**Evaluate:** Does not count setup as realized value. Distinguishes a person from an account. Does not infer payment from activity.

### 2. Audit a proposed tactic

Take a proposal such as a referral program or pricing change. State the diagnosed bottleneck, evidence, mechanism and adverse side effect.

**Deliverable:** A one-page decision memo with an explicit stop condition.

**Evaluate:** No invented benchmark. A leading indicator and a downstream guardrail.

### 3. Write a sharp but fair critique

Write a short critique of a common tactic, then add the conditions under which that same tactic would be sensible.

**Deliverable:** A 200-word argument with one concrete example.

**Evaluate:** Distinguishes example from evidence. Does not ridicule users or coworkers.
